## About This Module

An agent with a connected folder can compare documents, build a matrix, and compute on a
spreadsheet, and it will do all three fluently. Synthesis still fails in two familiar ways: a gap in
the evidence gets filled from somewhere other than the evidence, or a clean narrative erases the
disagreement that made the evidence worth reading.

This module works from a deliberately incomplete evidence log and a single year of usage data. The
files are small enough to hold in your head, which is the point — you can tell when a description of
them is wrong. Everything you produce is a file in the folder, so the analysis can be audited a row
at a time by someone who was not in the conversation.

If you audited the research scan in Module 2, you have met these five records already from the other
direction. Bring that with you.

## What You'll Learn

- How to keep analysis inside supplied evidence, and how to test where that boundary sits
- How to reason from a single boundary test to how much of the rest of an output you can trust
- How to build a claim-evidence matrix whose verification statuses mean one thing throughout
- How to separate direct evidence of use from proxies for attention and access
- How to preserve disagreement, heterogeneity, and missing perspectives in a synthesis
- How to require formulas and assumptions in a data analysis, and how to spot-check it yourself
- Why a defensible description beats a recommendation the data cannot support

## Skills in This Module

You will **modify** a skill. The review-ai-research-output skill you used in Module 2 carries a short controlled
vocabulary of verification statuses; in the second exercise you take those statuses out of the skill,
cut them down to language your team would actually use, add the mapping this evidence log needs, and
save the result as a file in the connected folder. Everything after that is built on your version
rather than the original.

The thing to notice is how little is involved. A skill is Markdown — headings, lists, definitions in
plain sentences — and adapting one takes a text editor and a view about how your colleagues talk. In
Module 4 you will write one from nothing, and this is the step that makes that a small move rather
than a cold start.

## Archives track

Archives staff work the same three problems — bounded evidence, a synthesis that keeps its
disagreements, and arithmetic with its assumptions on the surface — on
`sample-data/archives/accession-note.txt` and `sample-data/archives/digitization-inventory.csv`. You
will appraise the accession note one body of material at a time, separating what it establishes from
what it leaves open, and build a processing plan that stays defensible without pretending the open
questions are settled. You will count the digitization inventory, compute on it with every formula
and denominator shown, and reproduce some of that arithmetic yourself.

Your outputs are `outputs/archives-description-standard.md` (your modified rubric),
`outputs/archives-evidence-matrix.xlsx`, `outputs/archives-processing-plan.md`, and
`outputs/archives-digitization-analysis.md`. Follow the `## Archives track` section in each exercise.
