**Measuring readership beyond the download**

Lindgren, A.

*Bibliometrics and Information Practice*, vol. 16, no. 2 (2024), pp. 155–171
Article type: research article

## Abstract

Download counts are the most widely reported measure of the reach of open research, and they are the
weakest. We paired server-log retrieval counts for 4,300 deposited health articles with three
independent measures of what happened next: a reading-behaviour panel of 1,100 consenting users,
survey-reported use by 890 practitioners, and citation and guidance-document appearances. Retrieval
counts correlated only weakly with panel-measured reading (rho = 0.21) and not at all with
survey-reported use (rho = 0.04). Between 34% and 41% of retrievals in our sample were followed by no
measurable engagement with the file. Automated and duplicate retrieval, batch harvesting by discovery
services, and repeat retrieval by the same user each inflate counts in ways that standard robot
filters do not remove, and we show that the inflation is not uniform across access categories, so
open and closed comparisons drawn from retrieval counts are not comparable. We also find no
reliable way to infer a retriever's sector or occupation from repository logs, and we therefore
recommend against reporting download counts as evidence of readership, of use, or of reach among any
particular audience.

**Keywords:** downloads, readership, altmetrics, repository logs, measurement validity
