**Preprints and public health communication during health emergencies**

Duarte, C.

*Science Communication Today*, vol. 7, no. 4 (2022), pp. 310–329
Article type: research article

## Abstract

During the acute phase of a health emergency, findings frequently reach practitioners, journalists,
and the public as preprints - manuscripts posted to a server before peer review. This study analyses
610 preprints posted during three declared health emergencies and traces how each was described in
subsequent news coverage and agency briefings. We find that the preprint status of a finding was
stated in 31% of news items and in 44% of agency briefings, and that 68 of the 610 preprints were
substantially revised or withdrawn before publication, including 9 that had already been quoted in
guidance. We discuss the timing dilemma facing communicators during an emergency, the practices that
made preprint status legible to non-specialist readers, and the retraction and correction
infrastructure that preprint servers currently lack.

Our scope is the speed and framing of emergency communication; questions of licensing, publication
charges, and journal business models are left to other work.

**Keywords:** preprints, science communication, health emergencies, peer review
