**Open access and the uptake of public health evidence: a systematic review**

Ahmed, S.; Lindqvist, P.

*Evidence Synthesis Quarterly*, vol. 18, no. 1 (2025), pp. 1–34
Article type: systematic review · Received 14 Mar 2024 · Published 8 Jan 2025

## Abstract

We searched six databases for studies published 2010–2024 examining whether open-access availability
affects the uptake of health research, and included 41 studies. Outcomes were highly varied:
17 studies measured citations, 11 measured downloads or page views, 8 measured self-reported reading,
and 5 measured a change in practice. Pooling was not appropriate for most comparisons. Where pooling
was possible, heterogeneity was high (I² = 82%, 95% CI 74–88), and prediction intervals crossed the
null for every outcome except citations. Thirty-four of the 41 included studies were conducted in
biomedical or clinical research settings; 4 were in public health, 2 in health policy, and 1 in
health economics. Thirty-eight were conducted in high-income countries. Certainty of evidence was
rated low for citation outcomes and very low for reading and practice outcomes. We conclude that the
direction of effect is inconsistent across outcome types and that the present evidence base does not
support generalisation beyond biomedical research in high-income settings.

**Keywords:** open access, research uptake, systematic review, heterogeneity
