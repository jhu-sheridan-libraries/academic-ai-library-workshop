## Ferris Family Papers

**Collection number:** MS-114
**Repository:** Calderwood Institute Archives and Special Collections, Weldon Harbor
**Dates:** 1912–1968
**Extent:** 14 boxes
**Language:** English
**Copyright:** The Institute holds all rights to material in this collection.

### Biographical Note

Harlan Ferris was born in Weldon Harbor in 1889, the son of a shipwright, and was educated locally
before joining the city engineer's office as a draughtsman. He rose steadily and served as City
Engineer of Weldon Harbor from 1931 until his retirement. He was widely regarded as the most
capable municipal engineer of his generation on the eastern shore, and he is generally credited
with authoring the 1954 waterfront plan that reshaped the lower city. He married Ada Whitcomb in
1921. Their daughter, Ada Ferris, was active in civic affairs. Harlan Ferris died in 1968, mourned
by the city he had rebuilt.

### Scope and Content

The papers document the long and distinguished career of Harlan Ferris, City Engineer, and reflect
the wide range of his professional interests and civic involvements. Included are correspondence,
reports, drawings, photographs, clippings, and personal material. The collection is a rich source
for the study of municipal engineering and the growth of Weldon Harbor, and researchers will find
much of interest throughout. Family material is also present.

### Arrangement

The collection is arranged in four series.

- Series 1. Correspondence
- Series 2. Engineering reports and drawings
- Series 3. Miscellaneous papers
- Series 4. Photographs

Series 1 comprises 6.5 linear feet. Series 2 comprises 3 cubic feet. Series 3 and 4 are not
measured.

### Series Descriptions

**Series 1. Correspondence.** Incoming and outgoing letters, arranged chronologically. Includes
correspondence with the mayor's office, the harbor commission, and various contractors.

**Series 2. Engineering reports and drawings.** Reports, surveys, specifications, and blueprints
relating to municipal works, including the sewer extension, the seawall, and the program of slum
clearance in the blighted areas of the lower city.

**Series 3. Miscellaneous papers.** Material not otherwise classified.

**Series 4. Photographs.** Approximately 400 items. Views of municipal works under construction,
official portraits, and views of the colored district before clearance.
